# Dana's open-loop model vs Henry's model: differences and proposed changes

**Status (2026-10-26):** for discussion; nothing below is implemented yet beyond the five fixes already in `RobotSimulationOpenLoopModel_HenryConstants.ipynb` (geometry, load balance, rotating frame, exact current update, motor saturation).

- **Dana's model:** `dana-models/RobotSimulationOpenLoopModel.ipynb`. One fixed-step, forward-Euler loop with voltage input and a sequential pipeline: electrical, normal loads, stick/slip contact, chassis, then wheel speeds.
- **Henry's model:** `robot_model_voltage.ipynb` on `main` (`VoltageDrivenSlipSimulator`). One coupled ODE solved by Radau over each 10 ms hold, with a contact solve inside every evaluation.

Each row is one difference. **Proposal** is what the copy (`RobotSimulationOpenLoopModel_HenryConstants.ipynb`) would change to. Mark each row agree, disagree, or modify before anything is implemented.

| # | Topic | Dana | Henry | Proposal for the copy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Symbols** | ω = wheel speed; θ = robot heading; θ̇ = yaw rate | θ̇_i = wheel speed, θ_i = wheel angle; φ = heading; ω = yaw rate | Henry's: wheel speeds `theta_dot`, heading `phi`, yaw rate `omega`. Renaming only ω → θ̇ would clash with Dana's θ for heading, so heading has to become φ too. |
| 2 | **Body axes** | x forward, y left | x right, y forward | Keep Dana's (x forward, y left) in the copy; convert Henry's constants on input, as the copy does now. *Alternative:* switch to Henry's axes so both models read the same. |
| 3 | **Wheel geometry** | Tangential only: drive direction is the CCW tangent, every yaw lever is R_b. The original also had the left/right pairing bug (fixed in the copy). | Position (`WHEEL_POSITION_DEG`) and direction (`WHEEL_ALPHA_DEG`) set separately, so wheels can be non-tangential. Yaw lever is r_i × d_i; the roller axis a_i is the direction rotated −90°. | Add Henry's option: positions and directions from `robot_constants`. Then H = [d_x, d_y, r × d] and the yaw lever is no longer R_b. Default tangential (equal arrays), as now. |
| 4 | **States** | I(4), ω(4), body velocity (3), pose (3), previous acceleration (3), slip flags (4) | Pose, world velocity, yaw rate (6); θ̇(4); i(4); grip/slide blend λ(4); LuGre bristle load z(4); 22 in total | Keep Dana's state container and add only what the agreed rows need (for example z(4) if LuGre, row 8). |
| 5 | **Time stepping** | Explicit, fixed dt (1 ms), one pass per step | Implicit Radau, adaptive inside each 10 ms hold (Jacobian reused) | Keep Dana's explicit fixed step: fast, simple, and her controller-architecture plan assumes it. Note that LuGre (row 8) with Henry's 2 µm bristles is stiff, about 1.3·10⁴ rad/s against the rim inertia. Explicit LuGre would need dt ≲ 0.1 ms, or a semi-implicit bristle update. |
| 6 | **Mass and inertia** | diag(M, M, J_zz). The CoG is at the base centre. A sticking wheel's force is T_m/r: wheel inertia is left out. | M_b plus wheel inertia reflected through the rolling constraint, (J_w/R_w²) H Hᵀ, for every gripping wheel. Optional CoG offset (CAD: 10.2 mm forward) with the full planar mass matrix. | (a) Add wheel inertia to the sticking force (3% of the chassis share here, but it is what makes the wheel speed consistent). (b) CoG offset as an option, default centred as Dana has it. |
| 7 | **Normal loads** | Previous step's acceleration. The original formula broke the moment balance (fixed in the copy: rigid plane, N = c₀ + c_x x + c_y y). Noise: additive white Gaussian per step, σ = 0.15 N. | `WHEEL_LOAD_MODEL` = equal / static / quasi-static (same rigid-plane solution), driven by the previous step's contact force, with lift-off: a negative load is set to zero and the rest re-solved. Load vibration: multiplicative, correlated, sum kept at m·g; size ∝ out-of-roundness × total speed², times (1 + gain × filtered wheel acceleration). | Keep the rigid-plane loads (already in). Add lift-off re-solve. Replace the white noise with Henry's load vibration, switchable. Using acceleration vs contact force for the lag gives the same result (F = m a). |
| 8 | **Drive (d) friction and stick/slip** | Stick if (T_m − T_roll)/r ≤ μ_s N; otherwise slide at sign(F_req)·μ_k N (constant, no Stribeck). Re-sticks on force alone and snaps the wheel speed: this makes the ±130 rad/s braking chatter. | LuGre (default): bristle state, force continuous through zero slip, Stribeck μ_d(v) from μ_s to μ_k (`ALPHA_D`). Or the switch model (Leine et al.) with a breakaway band and grip/slide blend. A gripping wheel includes its inertia and a restoring term on existing slip. | **Decision needed:** (a) slip-velocity switch: stay sliding until the rim speed matches the ground, kinetic force opposing the slip velocity, μ_d(v) Stribeck. Cheap and explicit-friendly. (b) LuGre: Henry found it accurate, but it is stiff (row 5). |
| 9 | **Roller (a-axis) friction** | None: rollers are free | μ_a(v) law (0.2 → 0.322), always active, acting through the centre for tangential wheels | Add Henry's μ_a law. It is why Dana's robot reaches ~5 m/s² and Henry's ~3.8 m/s². |
| 10 | **Wheel losses** | C_rr·N·r·sign(ω): discontinuous at zero speed, chatters on coast-down | C_rr·N·R_w·tanh(θ̇/ε) + b_w·θ̇ (`WHEEL_LOSS_SMOOTHING_SPEED`, `WHEEL_BEARING_VISCOUS_DRAG`) | Henry's smoothed form plus bearing drag. |
| 11 | **Friction disturbance** | Additive white Gaussian on μ_s and μ_k every step (same draw), σ = 0.03 | Fixed per-wheel multiplicative offset per run (σ = 0.05, `ENABLE_FRICTION_OFFSET`); no time-varying friction noise (vibration acts on N, row 7) | Henry's switchable offset. Keep Dana's per-step noise as an option? |
| 12 | **Motor and driver** | Voltage in; forward-Euler current; no limits (fixed in copy: exact current update, bus and current clamps) | Same physics, with current as a state; driver clamps at ±R·I_max + K_e·θ̇ and the bus; any controller command passes through the plant's limits | Done. *Open:* FOC current-commanded option (controller sends i_ref, driver makes V). |
| 13 | **Controller interface** | Plant takes voltages; no internal loops. Dana plans a 2-level PID: inverse kinematics, then 4 wheel loops. | Plant takes voltages; the PID plans current and converts to V. Anti-windup reads the plant's applied command. | Keep Dana's interface (voltage in), and expose the applied voltage and current, so the controllers can do anti-windup. |
| 14 | **Tests** | Symmetric and asymmetric voltage patterns, high voltage, pos/neg/zero sequence; ideal vs noise | Verification suite (geometry, balances, energy, saturation, coast-downs, grip/slide transitions, angle sweep, drift) | Keep Dana's tests. Add the verification cell (already in) and a braking test that flags chatter. |

## Questions to settle

1. Symbols (row 1) and body axes (row 2): adopt Henry's?
2. Drive friction (row 8): slip-velocity switch (fast) or LuGre (accurate, needs a smaller step or a semi-implicit update)?
3. Roller friction (row 9) and wheel inertia in the sticking force (row 6a): add both?
4. Disturbances (rows 7 and 11): Henry's switchable models only, or keep Dana's white-noise options alongside?
5. Time step: keep 1 ms explicit as the plant step for Dana's controller work?
