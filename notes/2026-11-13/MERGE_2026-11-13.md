## The Pololu current limit equivalent to the robot's fuse (11-13)
- **The robot:** a 20 A battery fuse; the motors get 15 A of it (the Pi up to 3 A peak, the flyback 1 A, the rest share the other 5 A). It limits **battery** current, from the 16.8 V battery to the BLDCs.
- **The sim's motor** is the geared Pololu: 8.5 N per amp at the wheel, against 0.89 N per amp for the direct-drive BLDC. So a Pololu amp is not a BLDC amp, and the limit has to be converted through the same motion.
- **Conversion,** at every 1 ms of the sim (same floor force, same wheel speed): from the Pololu's torque balance, `r F + T_roll = Kt_p i_p − J_p ω̇`. The BLDC then needs `i_b = (Kt_p i_p − (J_p − J_b) ω̇) / Kt_b` at `V_b = R_b i_b + Kb_b ω`, so its battery current is `Σ V_b i_b / 16.8 V`. BLDC constants (archive/BLDC): R 0.24 Ω, Kt 0.0265, J 1.66e-5 kg·m².
- **Data:** LQR, the linearized MPC and the tuned PID on five training paths, with every current limit off, 87,060 samples. All values are totals over the four motors.

| Same motion | Pololu (sim) | BLDC (robot) |
|---|---|---|
| Total motor current, peak | 5.2 A | 27.6 A |
| Total motor current, mean while moving | 0.89 A | 7.9 A |
| Battery current, peak | 2.9 A (12 V) | 3.8 A (16.8 V) |
| Electrical power, peak | 34 W | 63 W |
| Electrical power, mean while moving | 3.2 W | 9.3 W |

- The robot needs about 2–3× the Pololu's power for the same motion: the BLDC needs 9.6× the current for the same force and has about 10× the copper loss per newton.
- **The equivalent limit depends on what the 15 A limits:**

| What the 15 A limits | Equivalent Pololu total |
|---|---|
| BLDC battery current (the fuse), same motion | **7.4 A**: BLDC battery current fitted as `0.229 S² + 0.343 S` of the Pololu total S; at standstill with an even split, 6.7 A |
| BLDC motor current, same motion | 1.8 A (the paired samples at 15 A of BLDC current) |
| BLDC motor current, same power | 5.7 A: the BLDC draws 23.9 W at 15 A, and the Pololu gives about 4.2 W per amp |

- **Decision (Henry: the fuse):** 7.4 A for the controllers (the MPCs' `total_current_limit` and the LQR's clip: `TOTAL_CURRENT_LIMIT = 7.4` in ControllerAnalysis). The plant keeps `I_total_max = 15` A for now (Henry, 11-13).
- The data reaches about 3.5 A of Pololu total current, so 7.4 A is an extrapolation along the physics' quadratic form. On these paths the robot would use at most 3.8 A of its 15 A, so the limit only acts on much harder motion.

## Dana's Coulomb MPC: the double-counted drive force fixed (11-13)
- **The problem:** her model already pushes the chassis with the motor force `Kt i / r`, and her disturbance added the floor friction force on top, so the drive force was counted twice. Her friction `−μ_k F_n tanh(v_slip / 5 mm/s)` also flipped sign on tiny slips, which made it chatter.
- **The fix (merged copy):** the floor force replaces the motor force. The floor passes the motor force up to the friction limit `μ_d(v_slip) F_n` (Stribeck, as the plant: μ_s at no slip, falling to μ_k), and the disturbance is `F_floor − F_motor`. That is zero while the wheels grip, and takes off the excess drive force while the motor asks for more than the floor can give. It is continuous in slip, with no sign flip.
- **Checked on Dana's waypoint path:**
  - voltage change per step 0.278 → 0.045 V
  - peak slip 1.16 → 0.57 mm/s
  - all three waypoints settled
  - identical to the simple MPC there, because the wheels never slide on that path
- It stays out of the waypoint test's controller list for now. Dana's LuGre MPC adds its bristle force on top of the motor force the same way; it is not in the tests and has not been changed.
